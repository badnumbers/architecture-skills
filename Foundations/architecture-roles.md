# Architecture roles

## Overview

This page describes the difference between various types of 'architect', namely:
- software architect
- solution architect
- enterprise architect

In summary, enterprise architects really have nothing to do with IT specifically, although the terms is occasionally mis-applied to an IT role. Solution architects work at a higher level than software architects and are concerned with how various systems work *together*, whereas the focus of a software architect is narrower and tends to look at the *internals* of applications.

## Software architect

This role tends to comprise:
- establishing ways of working for a team
- the requirements of an application
- expertise in coding
- code-level design patterns
- optimising the performance of individual applications
- working with a single team

## Solution architect

This role tends to comprise:
- business objectives and how they are met by software
- the requirements of the overall system of applications
- expertise in how applications talk to one another
- solution-level design patterns
- efficiency over the entire system
- working with multiple teams

## Enterprise architect

This is not an IT role. It is about studying or modelling the structure and functioning of business processes as opposed to just IT processes, although IT is part of that. That said, there is some crossover between enterprise architecture and the two other roles described above, and an understanding of enterprise architecture, and of ArchiMate, a tool for visualising enterprise architecture, can be helpful.
